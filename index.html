How to get the data (free, 15-min delayed)

  1. Open cboe.com → SPX → Options (or SPY, QQQ, NDX, GLD…).
  2. Set Options Range: All and Expiration: All (or a near-term subset for 0DTE work).
  3. Click Download CSV at the bottom of the table and drop the file here.
  4. Gold: do the same on Cboe → GLD → Options, type the GC price at that moment into "Futures price", then click Gold mode. Levels are converted by the ratio GC ÷ GLD.

Daily snapshot files (SPX_YYYY-MM-DD_gex.csv etc., saved by the scheduled download) already contain the matching futures price, so levels appear in ES / NQ / GC prices as soon as you drop one in.

For the intraday Classic chart: download a fresh CSV every few minutes and add each one with "load snapshots" (or drop several at once). Each file becomes one point in time; the price line, level lines, max-change table and playback are built from them. Snapshots stay in this tab only; reloading the page clears them.

Open interest is published once a day (overnight), so OI-based levels are fixed for the session. Volume-based levels change with every refresh. Re-download during the day to update Vol GEX.

Method · what each number means

GEX, the sign convention, and units

For every contract: GEX = Γ × OI × 100 × S² × 0.01. That is the dollar amount of the underlying that dealers must buy or sell for a 1% move in spot. Γ is recomputed with Black-Scholes from Cboe's implied vol at the current spot (falling back to Cboe's own gamma if IV is missing).

The naive dealer convention is used, as in GexBot Classic: customers are assumed to buy calls and sell puts. So dealers are long call gamma (+) and short put gamma (−). Positive net GEX means dealers hedge against the move (selling rallies, buying dips), which damps volatility. Negative net GEX means they hedge with the move, which amplifies it. This convention is a simplification: real positioning is not observable from public data.

Vol GEX swaps OI for today's traded volume. It shows where today's flow is concentrated, which matters most for 0DTE. Stable / Unstable: if OI GEX and Vol GEX have the same sign, the day's flow agrees with the standing positioning.

Zero gamma, majors, and the other levels

Zero gamma follows GexBot Classic's documented method: a natural cubic spline is fitted through the per-strike net GEX bars (by volume; by OI when there is no volume yet) and its zero crossing between the Major Negative and Major Positive strikes is reported. Profile flip is a second, independent measure: total OI GEX recomputed at hypothetical spot prices (±25%), crossing nearest spot. Above it dealers are modelled net long gamma, below it net short.

Major +1/+2: strikes with the largest positive net GEX (call walls, which often act as resistance or magnets). Major −1/−2: the largest negative net GEX (put walls, often support that can break into acceleration). Both are shown for OI and for volume, like GexBot's major_pos_oi, major_neg_oi, major_pos_vol, major_neg_vol.

25Δ risk reversal: IV of the 25-delta call minus IV of the 25-delta put on the nearest expiry in scope. Negative means puts are bid (normal equity skew); a rising RR signals put demand fading. DEX: net delta exposure of the selected contracts, in $ of underlying. P/C: put/call ratios by OI and by volume.

Futures conversion: enter the live futures price. For an index (SPX→ES, NDX→NQ) the additive basis (futures − index) is added to every level. For an ETF whose price is far from the futures (GLD→GC, SPY→ES, QQQ→NQ) every level is multiplied by the ratio futures ÷ ETF price instead.

Limits

Cboe's free chain is 15-minute delayed; OI is end-of-previous-day. GexBot uses intraday data feeds and proprietary adjustments, so values will not match exactly. This tool is for research and education, not trading advice.
